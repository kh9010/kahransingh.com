Keeping track

Reads what I said and holds onto what I took on.

A short film about this is coming.

It reads my own words back and holds onto what I took on. Remembering is not my job.

Four parts. Each one proposes. I confirm.

The miners

Four times a day something reads what I said: the chat with my assistant, the voice notes transcribed beside it, the meetings. Each turn gets one label. A spark. A task. A reminder. A note about how the system itself should behave. Or nothing, which is most of a day.

What has been read is kept as a set, not a high-water mark. A message can land late with an earlier time on it; a cursor would skip it forever and say nothing. A set cannot skip it.

The voice miner never reaches back more than two weeks on its own. The one time a backfill ran over years of meeting notes, nine of ten items were dead on arrival.

The judge

Nothing a miner proposes reaches me raw. One pass of judgment stands between the mine and my list. It drops what is already built, already tracked, stale or not actionable, and keeps what is new, open and doable.

A gate that keeps everything is not a gate. A candidate with no date and no way back to where it came from never reaches the judge. A possibility and a commitment are told apart before judgment.

What it keeps lands on the backlog marked as mined, with its source and date, where I clear or promote it. A kept idea goes to the garden instead. One that is both goes to both.

The Curator

One pass over the backlog every morning, before I am up. It finishes what the machine started and proposes what a person would. It does not decide.

Every morning it also re-checks the ground under an item. The file it points at, the branch, the change. When the world moved and the words did not, the item comes back up for a look.

The sparks garden

Ideas are kept apart from tasks by construction. A spark is held, not scheduled.

The garden never nags. It answers when I ask. It echoes a related seed when I add a new one. It offers once a week at most. A spark said out loud on a Tuesday used to evaporate on Tuesday. Now it is a seed with a date.

When a seed is ready to go somewhere, the system suggests a home and I confirm. The seed stays in the garden with a note of where it went.

Two lines of its constitution decided the shape.

  • Tenet 11 “Review-gate: the system proposes, Kahran confirms.”“Nothing unattended writes committed state.”

    The carve-outs are named, numbered and written into the constitution, so the code and the tenet cannot contradict.

  • Tenet 14 “One next move, an ORDER not options.”“Always a scaffold to react to, never a blank prompt.”

    The list is kept so that one thing can be handed to me, not so that I can read the list.

What it will never do

Never ticks the box. Never rewrites the list. Never puts a name on a person without my word. Never asks a third time. the rules the checker pins, in the order they were learned

The first version edited my list unattended, under a rule I had stated myself: that nothing on it was written by hand. That stopped being true the day I started ticking boxes and retitling things, so the machine’s write surface shrank to three kinds of act. Additions. Its own fields. Proposals.

This page is not my list. It is how the list is kept. The other four ideas are on the home page.
