The idea

Why this one

We're an active, curious family who like to walk, hike and explore — so the trip should be too. Northern Thailand is the rare place that's genuinely thrilling for every age at once.

It's easy enough to reach from both Delhi and the US, somewhere we haven't gathered before, and — crucially for late November — it lands right in the start of the north's cool, dry season: clear skies, mild days, low humidity. We keep it to two bases over four nights, with a single short, gentle travel morning in the middle, so the little ones and the grandparents are never on a long road.

What we're solving for

The brief, in order

  • 1Easy to get to — works for the Delhi twelve and the US eight, two little ones flying long‑haul.
  • 2All together — one resort, one table, everyone under the same roof as much as possible.
  • 3Active — we move. This is what ruled out a sit‑still beach trip.
  • 4Every generation — real adventure for the kids, independence for the nineteen‑year‑olds, ease for the elders.
  • 5Somewhere new — last time was Phuket; this is a different Thailand entirely.
Where we'd stay

Two bases, four nights

Nights 1–2 · Chiang Mai

Four Seasons Chiang Mai

Mae Rim valley · multi‑bedroom residences

Set in working rice paddies fifteen minutes from town. We take adjoining residences so we're all together, with pools, a cooking school, temples nearby and a night market for the older kids. The #1‑rated resort in Thailand — and very easy on the grandparents.

Nights 3–4 · Golden Triangle

Anantara Golden Triangle

Where Thailand, Laos & Myanmar meet · ~10‑room block

The showstopper — a small jungle resort built around an elephant camp. Walk‑your‑own‑elephant mahout mornings, hikes to three‑country viewpoints, Mekong longtail boats, and glass "jungle bubbles" you can sleep in beside the elephants. Largely all‑inclusive: once we're there, everything's handled.

Also on the table

Two strong alternatives

Each makes a different trade against the brief. Thailand still edges it for our dates — but here's the honest case for the other two.

Option BBali — one villa, all of us under one roof

The "never leave the compound" answer: a single 10‑plus‑bedroom estate, everyone together for the whole week, not a single internal move. Wonderful — but late November is the start of Bali's wet season, and the best single‑roof estates lean more pool‑and‑beach than hike‑and‑explore. Best if "one house, one week" is the thing that matters most.

Option CVietnam — the grand journey, north to south

The most adventurous of the three: Hanoi's old quarter, a night on a Halong Bay junk, the lantern streets of Hoi An, on toward Ho Chi Minh. It has the easiest flight from Delhi of all — direct, about 4½ hours. Two catches for our window, though: it's a three‑ or four‑base journey with several internal hops rather than one home — a lot of moving with a 2‑ and 4‑year‑old — and late November is peak rainy season on the central coast, with Hoi An prone to flooding. North and south shine in November; the middle doesn't. A spectacular 10‑day trip, waiting for a drier month.

For those who can stay longer

Extend your stay

The core gathering is the four nights — but the Golden Triangle is beautifully placed for an add‑on, and many of us are travelling a long way to get here. Tack any of these onto the front or back; the rest of the group flies home on the 29th. Easiest for those already in India, or anyone with a flexible week.

Top pick

Luang Prabang, Laos

A jewel, an hour away
+2–3 nights1h direct flight from Chiang MaiCalm & dry in NovAll ages

The natural extension — and an easy one. A tiny UNESCO town of gilded temples where the Mekong meets the mountains: the dawn alms ceremony, turquoise swimming pools at Kuang Si Falls, sunset from Mount Phousi, a gentle night market, an elephant sanctuary. Slow, soulful, and a world away from anywhere we've been — perfect for grandparents and kids alike. November is one of the best months to go.

Bangkok

On the way anyway
+2–3 nightsEveryone routes through itBig‑city energy

Since nearly every flight home connects through Bangkok, it's the easiest bolt‑on of all. The Grand Palace and Wat Pho early, longtail boats through the old canals, the river hop‑on boat, rooftop dinners, and IconSiam for the teenagers. A day or two is plenty — enough to feel the buzz without getting templed‑out.

The Andaman coast

A beach wind‑down
+3–4 nightsKrabi / Phang NgaDry side in Nov

If we want to end soft and horizontal, the Andaman side (Krabi, Phang Nga — skip Phuket itself, we've done it) is sunny and calm in late November, while the Gulf islands are still rainy. Limestone cliffs, island‑hopping, warm water and not much to do but swim. The relaxed counterweight to a busy week of jungle.

Carry on to Japan

For the long‑haulers

For anyone with real time — and following the family already heading to Japan afterward — it's an easy onward hop from Bangkok. A different climate and a different world: early‑winter Kyoto and Tokyo. The ambitious add‑on, if the calendar allows.

Good to know

The practical bits

· Weather

Late Nov is the north's sweet spot — cool, dry days around 18–28°C, cooler evenings. Pack a light layer for the jungle mornings.

· What to bring

Walking shoes, swimwear, modest cover for temples (shoulders & knees), and a hat. Everything else the resorts handle.

· Visas

Thailand is visa‑free or e‑visa for most of us — Indian and US passports both straightforward. We'll send the exact steps once it's booked.

· Time zone

Thailand is GMT+7 — 1½ hrs ahead of India, ~12 ahead of the US East Coast. Gentle jet lag from Delhi, a real one from the States.

· Money

Thai baht; cards everywhere at the resorts. The Anantara stay is largely all‑inclusive, so wallets mostly stay shut.

· With kids

Cribs, nannies and kids' clubs are standard at both resorts. Under‑12s stay free in their parents' room at the Anantara.
