The moments that make it ours

The reason it's these exact dates

We arrive into the lantern festival

Yi Peng — Chiang Mai's mass sky‑lantern release — falls on our very first nights. And there's a right way to do it: not the 25,000‑seat coach‑shuttle event, but Yee Peng Lanna International at Mae Jo — the original, ~3,000 seats, religious ceremony first, sells out within days of release. We grab a block the day sales open for the adults and teens, and stage a private riverside krathong release for the toddlers and grandparents the same night.

✓ Official festival dates: 24–26 Nov 2026 · Loy Krathong falls 25 Nov

Fri 27 Nov

Kahran's birthday

A private hilltop dinner, the elephants below.

  1. The dawn walk, with the man who started it all

    The camp's herd belongs to the GTAEF — the foundation run by John Roberts, who invented the "rescue the elephant and employ the mahout's whole family" model every ethical camp now copies. The ask we place with our ten‑room block: on the birthday morning, Roberts or his head biologist walks the herd out with us at dawn, before the camp opens. Every Aman has a spa. Nobody has this.

  2. "Walking with Giants" — never riding

    The camp's signature, by name: two hours through the jungle alongside the herd, their mahouts, and a vet or biologist. 60+ rescued elephants; the kids train as junior mahouts. Nobody perches on a chair on an elephant's spine.

  3. A baci at Ban Khok Phou, three generations deep

    By boat to a 200‑year‑old Lao village, where the elders tie the white threads of a baci around our wrists — oldest hands first. The grandparents' headline moment: seated, honored, central. Not a photo op.

  4. "Dining by Design" — one long table by the baby elephant camp

    The resort's private dinner, in its most‑requested spot: a single long table set beside the nursery as the sun goes down. The birthday night that's no one else's.

  5. Three countries in a single glance

    Sundowners by longtail on the Mekong at the exact point Thailand, Laos and Myanmar meet — a drink as the light goes down over three borders.

  6. Sleep beside the elephants

    For whoever's brave: a glass "jungle bubble" out in the grassland, the herd grazing a few feet away under the stars.

The early days

The early days, three ways

This is how most of us will actually travel — out Friday the 20th, landing the 22nd, with two or three unhurried days before the core. Not an add‑on; the default. Here's what to do with them.

+2–3 nights · best for the festival

Chiang Mai, early

Arrive ahead and catch the full Yi Peng — the big organised lantern releases run the 24th–25th. Settle in, shake off the flight, be fresh when the family lands.

+2 nights · on the way up

Bangkok stopover

Break the long‑haul where you already connect — Grand Palace, the river, one good night out — then the short hop north. More on Bangkok →

+2 nights · land softly

A wellness reset

A spa day or two in the Chiang Mai hills to arrive calm and adjusted before the group energy — lovely for the grandparents especially.

For those who can stay longer

Extend your stay

The core is the four nights; the rest fly home on the 29th. But the Golden Triangle is well placed for an add‑on — easiest for anyone already in India or with a flexible week.

Top pick

Luang Prabang, Laos

+2–3 nts1h flight from Chiang MaiDry in NovAll ages

A tiny UNESCO town where the Mekong meets the mountains: dawn alms, the turquoise pools of Kuang Si Falls (pictured), sunset from Mount Phousi, an elephant sanctuary. Slow and soulful — perfect for grandparents and kids alike. The natural extension, and an easy one.

Bangkok

+2–3 ntsOn the route home

The easiest bolt‑on — everyone connects through it. Grand Palace and Wat Pho early, longtail canals, rooftops, IconSiam for the teens. A day or two is plenty.

Andaman coast

+3–4 ntsKrabi / Phang NgaDry side in Nov

End soft and horizontal. The Andaman side is sunny in late Nov (the Gulf isn't): limestone cliffs, island‑hopping, warm water. Skip Phuket — we've done it.

Also possible for the few with real time: carry on to Japan (an easy hop from Bangkok, following the family already heading there).

Good to know

The practical bits

· Weather

Cool, dry, ~18–28°C. A light layer for jungle mornings.

· Bring

Walking shoes, swimwear, temple cover (shoulders & knees), a hat.

· Visas

Visa‑free / e‑visa for Indian & US passports. Steps to follow once booked.

· Time

GMT+7 — 1½h ahead of India, ~12h ahead of the US East Coast.

· Money

Thai baht; cards everywhere. Anantara is largely all‑inclusive.

· Kids

Cribs, nannies, kids' clubs standard. Under‑12s free at Anantara.
