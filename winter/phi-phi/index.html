Why the plan changed

The brief, updated honestly

The Chiang Mai plan was built around doing things — lanterns, elephants, a schedule. When the family actually weighed in, the real brief surfaced: a relaxed resort with a pool and a beach, everyone in one place, and not much more than that. Fair enough. This is that plan, done properly.

Two more things it gets right. It's one base, zero internal moves — you unpack once. And it's honestly priced: a beach-bar Chang runs a couple of dollars, not $12 — so nobody hesitates before ordering the next round, whoever's paying. The signature stuff below is optional, cheap, and mostly involves a boat.

The geography

A small island in a calm sea

Fly into Phuket, an hour's drive to the marina, then a 45–60 minute private speedboat straight onto the resort's sand. No cars on the island — and on the north coast, no crowds either.

Base (where we sleep) Optional boat day Private speedboat

The one big trick of this plan: Phi Phi has two personalities. Ton Sai, the ferry-pier village, is dense, loud and backpacker-central — that's the Phi Phi people warn you about. The north coast is a different world: three quiet resorts on their own beaches, reached by the resorts' own boats. We never touch the pier.

Why it still feels special

Relaxed doesn't mean forgettable

The one thing worth scheduling

Our own boats, our own bay — at 7am

Staying on Phi Phi is a structural cheat code: Maya Bay opens at 07:00, and the Phuket day-boats don't arrive until ~09:30. From our beach it's a 20–30 minute hop — we get the famous bay nearly empty. Two ways to run it: SAii's own private longtail flotilla (3,500 THB per 6-seat boat, 4 hrs — ~$390 for all four boats) or three of their private speedboats for the full day (~$1,050 total): Maya, the green water of Pileh Lagoon, Viking Cave, a Bamboo Island snorkel, grandparents in shade, toddlers napping. Book the earliest slot, not the last.

✓ SAii in-house charters: ฿3,500/longtail · ฿12,500/speedboat · + ฿400/adult park fee (cash)

  1. A reef off the sand

    Laem Tong has living coral right off the beach — waist-deep over reef at low tide, a swimmable drop-off beyond — so grandparents and the four-year-old snorkel from a standing start. One honest caveat: the tide swings hard here (wide flats at low tide, a thin beach at high), which is why the swim-all-day beach comparison matters below.

  2. Hin Klang — the secret snorkel upgrade

    A submerged coral plateau 2km straight off Laem Tong: anemone fields, sea fans, barracuda, the odd turtle and blacktip reef shark. One of southern Thailand's best snorkel sites, a short longtail hop from our towels — most visitors never hear of it.

  3. Krathongs on our own beach

    We arrive into Loy Krathong. Banana-leaf boats, candles, three generations wading in to set them afloat off our own sand — the quiet version of the festival. SAii runs krathong-making and a floating ceremony; we'll confirm which night.

  4. First scuba, cheapest place on earth to learn

    Phi Phi is where backpackers learn to dive because it's warm, calm and absurdly cheap: Discover Scuba runs ~฿3,800 (~$105), full PADI Open Water ~฿13,900. The 19-year-olds come home certified; the 10-year-old gets a pool "bubblemaker" intro at the dive centre. Late Nov = the year's best visibility.

  5. The viewpoint, for whoever's restless

    The twin-bay postcard climb: 45–60 minutes of hot jungle trail from Loh Ba Gao for the teens and early risers. Everyone else sees the photo at lunch and feels fine about it.

  6. Sunset longtails & fire shows

    Wooden longtails at golden hour, drinks aboard, karsts going purple — a few hundred baht at a time. Then beach fire shows after dinner, which are just… what happens here. Zero planning, maximum small-kid delight.

Two ways to do the north coast

Option B · the notch up

The honest read: Holiday Resort wins on kids' infrastructure, flat ground and price; SAii wins on the swimming beach, the marine biologists and polish. If the answer comes back "comfortable, not fancy" — Option A and done. (Villa 360 and Centara Villas share the coast but are built into hillsides — stairs everywhere, wrong for us.)

Getting there

Airport tarmac to beach towel, ~3½ hours

There are no roads to this — which is exactly why it stays quiet. A private charter moves all 20 of us door-to-sand in one smooth line:

11:30LandPhuket (HKT). Everyone aims for flights landing before ~1pm — boats don't cross after dark.
12:301 hrTwo private 10-seater vans meet us at arrivals, take all 20 bags, and drive across Phuket to the marina.
13:3030 minMarina. Staff load the luggage; the family has cold drinks and a bathroom stop.
14:00~50 minPrivate speedboat straight across the Andaman — bypassing the public ferry and Ton Sai pier entirely.
15:00—The boat pulls onto our beach. Cold towels, welcome drinks, bags go to the rooms without us.

The group-size cheat: SAii's fully private boat is ฿22,000 (~$620) each way for all of us — literally half the price of 20 shared-boat seats (฿1,800++ pp) — and it times itself to our arrival. Phi Phi Holiday runs its own shuttle from Ao Po Grand Marina (schedule on enquiry); 5 Star Marine quotes fully private Phuket charters with boats that seat all 20.

The Krabi back door

Anyone routing via Krabi (KBV) instead of Phuket has it even easier: a scheduled speedboat runs from Klong Jilad pier directly onto Laem Tong Beach — ฿1,150–1,250 pp including the airport pickup, ~90 minutes, and the airport-to-pier drive is shorter than Phuket's. Worth checking both cities' fares before anyone books.

Return flights out of HKT/KBV should depart after 2pm. Late Nov sits at the start of the calm season — crossings are at their smoothest — but shared boats are fixed-departure (last ~16:00): miss it and you overnight on the mainland, which is exactly why the private boat is worth it for the arrival day.

On and under the water

  • Leisure Dive Center — the PADI shop right on Laem Tong beach, serving all the north-coast resorts; SAii also runs its own PADI 5★ centre with a dive boat off the resort. Late Nov opens the year's best-visibility season.the teens' certification week
  • Bamboo Island — white-sand islet 15–20 min north, shallow easy snorkeling; the best kid-friendly boat stop.the 10-year-old's favorite hour
  • Mangrove & village stroll — the flat walk from SAii through Loh Ba Kao village along the stream to the mangroves: the grandparents' gentle morning loop.the flat-terrain option

Good to know

The practical bits

· Weather

Late Nov is the turn into dry season: ~30°C days, 29°C sea, calm crossings. The odd short afternoon shower is possible — it passes.

· The flight rule

The one hard constraint: land at HKT before ~1pm, fly out after 2pm. Boats don't cross in the dark.

· No cars

None on the island. Our end is boat-in, boat-out — the resort handles every bag.

· Tides

Laem Tong swings hard — reef-snorkeling at low tide, thin beach at high. Loh Ba Gao (SAii) swims at every tide; worth weighing in the resort vote.

· Mosquitoes

The resorts back onto jungle and a stream — real DEET at dusk, especially for the toddlers. Rash guards double as sting cover on jellyfish days.

· Money

Thai baht; cards at resorts, cash for the village, park fees and Jasmin — no real ATMs on our end. Stock the room fridges from mainland Phuket before the boat.

· Visas

Visa-free / e-visa for Indian & US passports, same as before.

· Quiet by design

The north coast goes silent by ~9pm and the clinic is in Ton Sai (serious cases boat to Phuket). For this trip, the silence is the point — just know it going in.

· Kids

Cribs and family rooms standard; the sea is bath-warm, and morning seas are the calmest for small stomachs on boats — longtails ride gentler than speedboats.
