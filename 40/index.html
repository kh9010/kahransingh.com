The receipts

This plan has history

October 2019: Andrew Wileman plants the idea — bike from northern Thailand to the Mekong, cross into Laos, roll into Luang Prabang. Jessica Pollett (now Seitz), event planner extraordinaire and dear friend, takes it to SpiceRoads and starts engineering a custom trip for 10–12 of us: 20–30km a day, "reasonably fit but not serious cyclists, nothing too, too strenuous."

"For my 33rd birthday (Nov 2020, please be there!) I am planning to cycle from Thailand to Laos, over a 4–5 night trip." — me, to Andrew, October 2019. Reader: nobody went anywhere in Nov 2020.

We even found the flaw before the pandemic did: the Chiang Rai → Luang Prabang route needs two days on the slow boat, which killed the fun ("Ugh. This seems not that fun."). Kie at SpiceRoads had already offered the fix in her last email — ride Chiang Mai → Chiang Rai instead, all riding, no boat — and then the world shut. That fix is now the plan.

The route

Chiang Mai → the Golden Triangle

Four to five riding days north through the mountains — support vehicle always trailing, so any day can end early with a beer in the truck. Shape, not gospel:

Day 0 · Chiang Mai

Assemble & bike fitting

Land, fit bikes, khao soi at a Michelin Bib Gourmand shophouse, early night. (Whoever comes early gets the temples and the old city.)

Day 1

Chiang Mai → the hot springs country ~40–50 km

Out of the valley through rice paddies and back roads, ending somewhere with hot springs and cold Chang.

Day 2

Into the hills ~40 km

The mountain day — climbs earned, descents savored, support truck for the honest. Night in a hill town.

Day 3

Tea country — Doi Mae Salong ~40 km

Ridgeline tea plantations settled by Yunnanese exiles; oolong tastings at altitude. The best views of the week.

Day 4

Down to the Mekong ~50 km

The long roll down to Chiang Saen's 700-year-old ruins and the river. First sight of Laos across the water.

Day 5 · the birthday

The Golden Triangle

Short victory lap to the tri-border point, then off the bikes: walking with the elephants at the Anantara camp, sundowners by longtail where Thailand, Laos and Myanmar meet, one long dinner.

Optional epilogue

Luang Prabang — by air, not slow boat

The 2020 lesson, learned: the 1-hour flight from Chiang Rai gets us the dawn alms, Kuang Si Falls and the Mekong sunsets without two days on a boat. +2–3 nights for whoever can.

If the dates align

The lantern festival

Yi Peng / Loy Krathong falls around mid-Nov 2027 (date TBC) — likely a week or so before the ride. Early arrivals could catch it in Chiang Mai; worth checking before fixing dates.

Anantara's elephant camp

"Walking with Giants" — two hours through the jungle beside 60 rescued elephants and their mahouts. Ends the ride the way nothing else could.

How it gets booked

Operators & the old thread

  • SpiceRoads — custom-trip desk, where the 2019 thread lives. Contact was Kie (Pornwadee Photchanabanphot), custom@spiceroads.com. They run private groups our size routinely; more riders = cheaper per head; ~30 days' notice on final headcount.the front-runner — we just reopen the thread
  • Grasshopper Adventures — the boutique alternative, strong in northern Thailand day rides and multi-day tours.worth a competing quote
  • Exodus / KE Adventure — UK group-tour operators Andrew recommended; scheduled departures if we'd rather join than charter.the fallback
  • Jessica — planned the Galapagos trip, the engagement party, and half of 2019–20. First message goes to her: "remember Chiang Rai? It's back on, for the 40th."the co-conspirator

Ballpark from 2019, inflated honestly: mid-range custom cycling tours run roughly $200–350/person/day with support vehicle, guides, bikes and hotels — call it $1.5–2.5k/person for the week before flights, less as the group grows. To firm up with quotes.
